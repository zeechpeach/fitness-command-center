Morning foundation primer 8 min · every day, workout or not

Feet, ankles, hips and pelvis. This is the block that changes the next decade, and it is the one you will be tempted to skip, so it runs before anything else while you are fresh. If time is short, cut the last exercise of the session instead of cutting this.

  1. Arch doming (short foot) — 2 × 10 per foot, 5 second holds. Lift the arch without curling the toes.
  2. Big toe press and toe spread — 2 × 10 per foot. The big toe is what drives the arch upward.
  3. Knee-to-wall ankle mobilization — 2 × 10 per side. Recovers the forward shin travel the sprains took. This is the gate on squat depth.
  4. Single-leg balance — 3 × 30 sec per side. Progress eyes open, then eyes closed, then on a folded towel. Highest-yield item for repeatedly sprained ankles.
  5. 90/90 breathing with a slight posterior tilt — 5 slow breaths. Ribs down, pelvis under you. The control side of the anterior tilt.
  6. Glute bridge, 3 second hold — 10 reps. Wakes the glutes so they contribute today instead of leaving the work to your lower back.

Pre-bed routine 10 min · last thing before sleep

Decompress the spine, lengthen what training shortened, and downshift the nervous system so you fall asleep faster. Evening is the right time for the long holds, because they would blunt your output if you did them before lifting. Keep the lights low and breathe through your nose the whole way through.

  1. Roll the arch on a ball — 60 sec per foot. Flat feet spend all day under load. This is where they get attention.
  2. Calf stretch on a step — 45 sec per side, straight knee then bent knee. Bent knee reaches the deeper calf muscle the straight-leg version misses.
  3. Half-kneeling hip flexor stretch — 60 sec per side, glute squeezed and ribs down. This supports the strength work rather than replacing it. Without the glute squeeze you are arching your lower back instead of stretching the hip.
  4. Supine figure-4 glute stretch — 60 sec per side. Ease into the left side and back off immediately if you feel pinching at the front of the hip.
  5. Constructive rest — 10 slow breaths, on your back, knees bent, feet flat. Lets the lower back and hip flexors let go without you stretching anything.
  6. Legs up the wall — 3 to 5 min, inhale 4 and exhale 6. Decompresses the spine and shifts you toward the parasympathetic side before you get into bed.
